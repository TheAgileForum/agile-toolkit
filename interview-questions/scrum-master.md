# Scrum Master interview questions — 15 scenarios and what panels listen for

Collected from real panels for Scrum Master and Agile TPM roles in 2025–2026.
Answer with STAR: situation, action, result — and a number where one exists.

## Role and mindset

**1. A hiring manager says you "run the meetings". How do you describe the role?**
Frame it as accountability for Scrum's effectiveness: coaching the team and the
organisation, removing impediments, facilitating events only as a means to inspect
and adapt. Close with one example where coaching changed behaviour outside a ceremony.
*Listens for:* whether you have a theory of the role or just a job description.

**2. Scrum Master vs Agile TPM — what is the difference in your experience?**
SM owns Scrum effectiveness and team health. Agile TPM usually owns cross-team
delivery outcomes, dependency risk and stakeholder reporting. The overlap is
facilitation and delivery hygiene. Be explicit about which hat you wore.
*Listens for:* honesty about your actual scope, not title inflation.

**3. Tell me about coaching without positional authority.**
Situation: team skipping refinement. Action: bring data plus a proposed working
agreement, not a mandate. Result: fewer carry-overs, clearer Sprint Goals.
*Listens for:* influence through evidence, escalation as a last resort.

**4. The Product Owner treats the backlog as a stakeholder request queue.**
Coach value ordering around Product Goal and outcome metrics; facilitate stakeholder
conversations so the PO stays accountable for ordering. Offer a backlog health
workshop, never take prioritisation over.
*Listens for:* respect for accountabilities; you cannot fix a PO by seizing their job.

**5. What does servant leadership look like on a bad week?**
Protect focus and psychological safety under pressure: clarify the Sprint Goal,
surface risk early, shield from thrash while giving stakeholders options.
*Listens for:* the difference between serving and absorbing the team's work.

## Events and facilitation

**6. Stakeholders keep adding must-haves during Sprint Planning.**
Restate capacity and the Goal. New asks go to the backlog with explicit trade-offs.
Offer a follow-up with the PO and requester if strategic. Never inflate commitment.
*Listens for:* a script you have actually used.

**7. Daily Scrum has become a status report to you.**
Stop answering as a manager. Coach the Developers to inspect progress toward the Goal
and replan 24 hours. Park status for a separate stakeholder sync.
*Listens for:* whether you can give up being the centre of information.

**8. How do you run a retro that produces change?**
One or two experiments, named owner, check-in date, evidence next sprint. Rotate
formats. Bring cycle time or escaped defects to ground the discussion.
*Listens for:* follow-through, not sticky-note vocabulary.

**9. Refinement for a mixed-experience team?**
Short and frequent; separate discovery from sizing; DoR clear before Planning; pair
juniors with seniors on slicing. You facilitate clarity, you are not the proxy PO.

**10. Fully remote, multi-timezone team — how do you facilitate?**
Async-first prep, tight timeboxes, rotating facilitation, a written decision log,
overlapping core hours for decisions. Record outcomes, not meetings.
*Listens for:* concrete practice, not tool name-dropping.

## Metrics and delivery

**11. Leadership wants velocity to go up every sprint.**
Velocity is a planning aid, not a KPI; rising velocity can just mean inflation.
Offer Sprint Goal success rate, cycle time, escaped defects, business outcomes.
*Listens for:* spine plus an alternative, not just refusal.

**12. Which flow metrics, and why?**
Cycle time and lead time for predictability, WIP for focus, throughput for capacity
conversations, aging WIP for stuck work. DORA signals for tech teams, never in
isolation from team health.

**13. The team keeps missing the Sprint Goal. Diagnostic approach?**
Check Goal clarity, capacity honesty, mid-sprint scope changes, dependency delays,
quality escapes. Use data plus a blameless retro. Fix the system before people.

**14. Team drowning in technical debt.**
Make debt visible as product risk; help the PO allocate capacity by impact; negotiate
an explicit percentage; track the debt burndown like any other work.

**15. How do you handle AI in delivery — what stays human?**
Name one workflow (drafting acceptance criteria, clustering retro themes, summarising
standups) and one thing you keep human (estimates, sentiment about people, conflict
decisions). Mention a tool you actually used and where it failed you.
*Listens for:* judgement boundaries, not enthusiasm.

## Closing questions worth asking them

- What does the team's Definition of Done look like today?
- Who owns the backlog, and how often does it get reordered?
- How did the last two sprints go wrong, in your view?

The last one tells you more about the culture than the job description does.
