# Retrospectives — six formats and a facilitation script

## The script that makes retros produce change

1. **Set the stage (5 min).** One sentence on why we are here: inspect how we worked,
   adapt one thing. No blame, no performance review.
2. **Gather data (15 min).** Silent writing first, then share. Silent-first protects
   the quiet half of the room.
3. **Generate insight (15 min).** Cluster, then ask "why" twice on the biggest cluster.
4. **Decide what to do (15 min).** Choose **one** experiment. It needs an owner, a
   check-in date, and a way to tell whether it worked.
5. **Close (5 min).** Read the action back. Confirm the next check-in.

Total 55 minutes. If a retro runs 90 minutes and ends with eight action items, it
produced nothing.

## Formats

### 1. Start / Stop / Continue (default, 45 min)
Three columns. Best when the team is stable and comfortable. Weakness: produces
generic actions ("communicate better"). Force specificity: an action must name an
observable behaviour.

### 2. 4Ls — Liked / Learned / Lacked / Longed for (50 min)
Good after a release or a rough sprint. "Lacked" surfaces missing support from
outside the team, which is usually where the real constraint sits.

### 3. Sailboat (60 min)
Wind (what pushes us), anchors (what holds us back), rocks (risks ahead), island
(the goal). Best for a team that has lost sight of purpose, or at the start of a
quarter.

### 4. Mad / Sad / Glad (40 min)
Fast, emotional, good for remote teams with low energy. Follow immediately with a
"what would fix the top sad item" round, or it stays a feelings exercise.

### 5. Timeline walk (60 min)
Walk the sprint day by day on a shared board, mark high and low points, then look for
the pattern. Best after an incident, a missed goal, or a crunch period.

### 6. Metrics-led (45 min)
Open with cycle time, lead time, WIP, escaped defects and Sprint Goal success. Ask
"what in our system produced this shape?" Not "who was slow". Use only metrics the
team already trusts, otherwise you get a debate about the metric.

## Rotating

Rotate format every sprint; rotate the facilitator every two or three sprints so
facilitation is a team skill, not a Scrum Master dependency. Put the format in the
calendar invite so people arrive in the right mode.

## Action quality test

An action is usable only if it passes all four:

- [ ] Observable: someone outside the team could see it happen
- [ ] Owned: one name, not "the team"
- [ ] Dated: a check-in, not "ongoing"
- [ ] Falsifiable: we will know if it failed

If it fails two or more, rewrite it before the retro ends.

## Remote facilitation notes

- Async prep the day before (board link + one prompt) raises quality more than any
  technique used live.
- Use breakout rooms for the gather phase above 8 people.
- Capture decisions in writing during the meeting, not after — a decision log beats
  a recording nobody rewatches.
- Watch for the two silent participants and ask them by name in the share round.

## Tracking

Keep one running page: sprint, experiment, owner, date, verdict (worked / partly /
did not). Revisit the last three at the start of every retro. A team that can see its
own experiments working will keep doing retros without being chased.
